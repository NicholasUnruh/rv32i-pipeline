# Fitting the final core on the Cyclone V

## Question

With the 8 KB data cache chosen in step 8, does the core still fit and meet timing on the DE1-SoC's Cyclone V, and what limits its clock now?

## Concept

**Fmax and the critical path.** The fitter places every cell, and the timing analyzer finds the slowest register-to-register path at each process, voltage and temperature corner. Fmax is the inverse of that path's delay. The slow 85 °C corner is the one that limits it.

**Tool front ends differ.** Verilator, Icarus, Yosys and Quartus each parse SystemVerilog themselves. Code that three of them accept can still fail in the fourth, so a design isn't portable until every tool in the flow has compiled it.

## What I tried

- Ran `make build` on the step-8 core. Analysis failed on the data cache's per-way generate loop.
- Reduced it to a one-module Quartus project and tried three forms. Only a separately declared `genvar` with `generate ... endgenerate` passed.
- Rewrote the loop in that form, then reran the tests, the C benchmark and the cache-sim crosscheck. The crosscheck CSV came out byte for byte the same.
- Compiled again, then asked the timing analyzer for the 20 worst setup paths.
- Simulated the board top with its RAM on the C benchmark.

## Result

- The core fits in 8% of the ALMs and meets timing at 50 MHz on every corner. Fmax is 81.47 MHz, up from 67.61 MHz at step 5.
- The step-5 critical path, which ran into the data RAM's byte enables, is gone, because stores now write in MEM from registers. The new one runs from the MEM address through the valid bits, the way hit and `stall` into the BTB's update enable.
- The cache's HDL needed one change for Quartus and none for behaviour.

## Next

Register the BTB update one cycle later to take `stall` off the critical path. The bigger performance lever is still the I-cache (73.6% of Embench's lost cycles). Loading the bitstream onto a board is still to do.
